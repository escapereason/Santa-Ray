## ADA COMPLIANCE REQUIREMENTS

### Critical ADA Standards for Sidewalks

**Oakland Municipal Code + ADA Requirements:**

| Requirement | Specification | This Project | Compliance Method |
|-------------|---------------|--------------|-------------------|
| **Maximum Vertical Offset** | ≤ ¼ inch between sections | Currently EXCEEDS ¼" | Complete replacement eliminates trip hazards |
| **Running Slope** | 1-2% for drainage (max 2%) | Verify with level | Proper grading during installation |
| **Cross Slope** | Maximum 2% | Verify with level | Bull float and hand trowel technique |
| **Minimum Width** | 48 inches (4 feet) | This section is 48" | Meets minimum exactly |
| **Surface** | Stable, firm, slip-resistant | Broom finish concrete | Standard compliant finish |
| **Control Joints** | Every 5-6 feet | 28ft section = 5 joints | Prevents random cracking |

## CONCRETE SPECIFICATIONS

### Mix Design

**Standard Sidewalk Mix:**
- **Strength:** 3,000-4,000 PSI (pounds per square inch)
- **Type:** Air-entrained concrete (freeze-thaw resistance)
- **Slump:** 4-6 inches (workability for finishing)
- **Aggregate:** 3/4" maximum size

**Why Air-Entrained:**
- Freeze-thaw resistance (important for Oakland winters)
- Improved workability for finishing
- Better durability over time
- ADA-compliant surface texture

### Thickness & Reinforcement

**Thickness:** 4 inches (Oakland standard)
- Adequate for pedestrian traffic
- Industry standard for residential sidewalks
- Sufficient strength for occasional light vehicles (wheelchairs, strollers)

**Reinforcement:**
- Wire mesh (6"x6" W1.4xW1.4) OR
- Rebar grid (#3 rebar @ 18" on center both ways)
- **Purpose:** Crack prevention, structural integrity
- **Placement:** Middle third of slab depth (~2" from bottom)

### Finishing Technique

**Broom Finish (Standard):**
- Bull float for initial leveling
- Hand trowel for smoothness
- Edger for rounded edges
- Groover for control joints
- **Broom for slip resistance** (final step)

**Why Broom Finish:**
- ✅ Slip-resistant (ADA requirement)
- ✅ Durable surface
- ✅ Low maintenance
- ✅ Standard for Oakland sidewalks

### Cure Time Details

**Why Cure Time Matters:**
- Concrete gains strength through hydration process
- Too-early loading causes permanent damage
- Proper curing ensures 50+ year lifespan
- ADA compliance requires full structural integrity

**Cure Timeline:**

| Time After Pour | Strength Gain | Activity Allowed |
|-----------------|---------------|------------------|
| **4 hours** | ~20% | None - surface is soft |
| **24 hours** | ~50-60% | Light foot traffic, form removal |
| **3 days** | ~70-80% | Normal pedestrian use |
| **7 days** | ~90% | Full use, no restrictions |
| **28 days** | 100% | Design strength achieved |

**Curing Methods:**
1. **Curing Compound** (most common) - Spray-on membrane retains moisture
2. **Plastic Sheeting** - Covers concrete to prevent moisture loss
3. **Water Curing** - Keep surface wet for 3-7 days (uncommon for sidewalks)

**This Project:** Curing compound + barricades for 24 hours minimum

## WEATHER & SEASONAL CONSIDERATIONS

### Temperature Requirements

**Optimal Concrete Placement:**
- **40-90°F** during placement and first 24-48 hours
- Below 40°F: Requires heated concrete and insulated curing
- Above 90°F: Requires evaporation retarder and frequent watering

### Cold Weather Concerns (Below 50°F)

**Issues:**
- Slower strength gain
- Extended cure time required
- Risk of freeze damage if temperature drops below 32°F

**Solutions:**
- Use heated ready-mix concrete
- Insulated blankets or curing blankets
- Extend cure time to 48-72 hours
- Additional cost: $100-300 for cold weather measures

### Hot Weather Concerns (Above 85°F)

**Issues:**
- Rapid evaporation causes surface cracking
- Reduced working time for finishing
- Potential strength reduction

**Solutions:**
- Work early morning (coolest part of day)
- Evaporation retarder spray
- Frequent light water misting during cure
- Additional cost: $50-150 for hot weather measures

### Rain Considerations

**Before Pour:**
- ✅ Light rain OK if base is not saturated
- ⚠️ Heavy rain may delay 1 day (saturated base)
- ❌ Pour should not proceed if rain expected within 24 hours

**During Pour:**
- ❌ **STOP WORK** if rain begins
- Plastic sheeting available for emergency protection
- Light mist acceptable, heavy rain ruins surface

**After Pour (First 24-48 hrs):**
- ⚠️ Light rain may be OK if curing compound applied
- ❌ Heavy rain can damage fresh concrete surface
- Protection with plastic sheeting critical

**Seasonal Recommendation:**
- **Best:** May-October (dry season in Oakland)
- **Avoid:** November-March (rainy season)
- **This Project:** CRITICAL - cannot wait for ideal weather due to escrow

## WARRANTY & LONG-TERM MAINTENANCE

### Typical Warranty

**Workmanship Warranty:** 1-2 years (verify with Challenge Rooter)
- Covers installation defects
- Cracking beyond normal (hairline cracks normal)
- Settlement or heaving issues
- Surface deterioration

**What's NOT Covered:**
- Normal wear and tear
- Damage from tree roots
- Damage from heavy vehicles
- Cosmetic staining
- Freeze-thaw damage (rare in Oakland)

**Get in Writing:** Warranty terms, duration, claim process

### Long-Term Maintenance

**Routine Maintenance:**
- **Cleaning:** Pressure wash annually to remove dirt/algae
- **Sealing:** Consider concrete sealer every 3-5 years (optional)
- **Crack Repair:** Seal cracks >⅛" wide to prevent water intrusion
- **Joint Maintenance:** Re-seal control joints if deteriorated

**Prevent Damage:**
- Keep tree roots at least 3-5 feet from sidewalk
- Avoid heavy vehicle parking (>3,000 lbs)
- Clear ice/snow promptly (rare in Oakland)
- Address drainage issues immediately

**Expected Lifespan:**
- Well-maintained concrete sidewalk: **30-50 years**
- Oakland sidewalks: 20-40 years typical (tree root issues common)
- This replacement with proper base: 30+ years expected
