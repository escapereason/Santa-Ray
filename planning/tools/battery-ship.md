# Battery Transportation Options - Air Travel

## Overview
Since we're shipping tools without batteries to avoid hazmat fees, this document outlines options for transporting the 2x Ryobi 18V lithium-ion batteries via commercial airline travel.

## TSA/FAA Regulations for Lithium Batteries

### Carry-On Luggage (RECOMMENDED)
**✅ ALLOWED**: Lithium-ion batteries in carry-on bags
- **Capacity Limit**: Up to 100 watt-hours (Wh) per battery
- **Quantity Limit**: Reasonable personal use quantities
- **Condition**: Must be for personal use, not commercial transport

### Checked Luggage  
**❌ PROHIBITED**: Spare lithium-ion batteries in checked bags
- **Exception**: Batteries installed in devices may be checked
- **Risk**: Fire hazard in cargo hold without crew access

**❌ 6.0Ah batteries (P107/P108/P193) EXCEED TSA limits**

- **Recommendation**: Ship these separately via ground transport

## Air Travel Transportation Plan

### Option 1: Carry-On Transport (BEST)
**Requirements:**
- Batteries must be in carry-on luggage
- Keep batteries in original protective covers/cases
- Terminals should not touch metal objects
- Declare at security if asked

**Process:**
1. Place batteries in clear plastic bag
2. Use terminal covers or tape over contacts
3. Pack in easily accessible part of carry-on
4. Be prepared to remove for separate screening

### Option 2: Installed in Device
**Requirements:**
- Battery must be installed in the actual tool
- Tool can be in checked luggage
- Battery cannot be removed during flight

**Process:**
1. Install one battery in drill or impact driver
2. Pack tool with battery in checked luggage
3. Carry spare battery in carry-on
4. Tool may require additional TSA screening

## Airline-Specific Policies

### Major Airlines (2025 Rules)
Most US airlines follow TSA guidelines:
- **American Airlines**: Comply with TSA rules
- **Delta**: Up to 100Wh in carry-on allowed
- **United**: Follow FAA/TSA regulations
- **Southwest**: Personal use batteries under 100Wh OK

### International Considerations
If traveling internationally:
- **IATA Rules**: Generally similar to TSA
- **Country-Specific**: Check destination country rules
- **Documentation**: Keep battery specs available

## Practical Travel Instructions

### For the Traveler (Person Flying)
**Before Packing:**
1. **Verify battery model and watt-hours**:
   - Check label on battery for model number
   - Confirm watt-hours: P102=27Wh, P103=36Wh, P104=72Wh
   - **STOP if 6.0Ah models (P107/P108/P193)** - these exceed TSA limits
2. Ensure batteries are not damaged or swollen
3. Check that terminals are clean and not corroded

**Packing Instructions:**
1. Use original battery cases if available
2. If no cases, tape over terminals with electrical tape
3. Place in clear ziplock bag
4. Pack in easily accessible part of carry-on
5. Bring this document for reference if questioned

**At Airport Security:**
1. Remove batteries if requested for separate screening
2. Explain they're tool batteries under 100Wh
3. Show specifications if needed (27Wh each)

### Documentation to Carry
- Photo of battery label showing voltage/capacity
- This document (print or on phone)
- Tool manual excerpt showing battery compatibility

## Cost Analysis: Flying vs. Shipping Batteries

### Flying with Batteries
- **Cost**: $0 (just carry them)
- **Time**: Immediate (with next trip)
- **Risk**: Very low (TSA compliant)

### Shipping Batteries Separately
- **Cost**: $48+ hazmat fee + shipping
- **Time**: 5-7 days
- **Risk**: Regulatory complications

**Conclusion**: Flying with batteries saves $50+ and is faster

## Alternative Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Regular Visit Planned
If someone is already planning to visit California:
- Add batteries to their carry-on
- Zero additional cost
- Batteries arrive with traveler

### Scenario 2: Special Trip for Batteries
- Only cost-effective if someone wants to visit anyway
- Round-trip flight costs far exceed battery value
- Not recommended for batteries alone

### Scenario 3: Mail Forwarding Service
Some services accept batteries and forward via ground:
- Research "battery shipping services"
- May still incur hazmat fees
- Verify they accept lithium-ion types

## Safety Considerations

### Battery Condition Check
Before travel, verify:
- [ ] No physical damage (cracks, dents)
- [ ] No swelling or bulging
- [ ] Terminals clean and not corroded
- [ ] Battery holds charge normally

### Damaged Battery Warning
**DO NOT TRAVEL** with damaged batteries:
- Cracked cases
- Swollen/puffy appearance  
- Excessive heat generation
- Corroded terminals
- Any signs of leakage
