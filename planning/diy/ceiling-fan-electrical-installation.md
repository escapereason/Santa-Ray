## YOUR SPECIFIC INSTALLATION PLAN

### Your Plan Overview

**Access:** Attic next to bedroom (accessible)

### Step-by-Step Plan for Your Situation

#### Phase 1: Understand the Existing Light Circuit

Before you can tap into the existing light, you need to understand how it's wired.

**Common Light Wiring Scenarios:**

**Scenario A: Power to Switch First (Most Common)**
- Power comes from panel to wall switch
- Switch sends hot wire to light fixture
- Light fixture has: Hot (from switch), Neutral (from panel), Ground

**Scenario B: Power to Light First**
- Power comes from panel to light fixture first
- Light fixture sends power down to switch
- Switch returns hot wire back to light

**How to Determine Which You Have:**

1. **⚠️ TURN OFF POWER** to light circuit at breaker
2. **Remove light fixture** from ceiling:
   - Remove cover/shade
   - Unscrew fixture from ceiling box
   - Lower fixture to expose wiring (don't disconnect yet)
3. **Count the cables** entering the light box:
   - **ONE cable (2-3 wires):** Scenario A - Power to switch first
   - **TWO cables (4-6 wires):** Scenario B - Power to light first, OR light is middle of circuit feeding other lights

**What This Means:**

- **Scenario A:** You'll need to run new cable from switch (not ideal - that's the door frame route you wanted to avoid)
- **Scenario B:** PERFECT - You can tap power at the light box and run new cable to fan

**If you have Scenario B (power at light):**
- ✅ This is ideal for your plan
- You can tap constant power at the light box
- Run cable through attic to fan location
- Install separate switch for fan (or use wireless switch)

#### Phase 2: Fishing Cable with Ruler Method

Your plan to use a ruler is clever and works well for short runs.

**Tools for Ruler Method:**
- **Flat metal ruler** or **metal tape measure** (stiff enough to push but flexible)
- Alternatively: **Glow rod segment** or **wire coat hanger**
- Flashlight/headlamp
- String or thin cord
- Tape

**Step-by-Step Fishing Process:**

1. **Mark Fan Location from Below:**
   - Measure from walls to find exact fan center
   - Drive small finish nail up through ceiling at center point
   - This nail will be your target from attic

2. **From Attic:**
   - Locate the nail from above
   - Locate the existing light fixture box
   - Measure straight-line distance (verify it's short run as expected)
   - Clear any insulation along the path

3. **Install Fan Box First:**
   - Position ceiling fan-rated box at nail location
   - Install adjustable fan brace between joists ($15-25)
   - Secure fan box to brace
   - **Don't cut ceiling opening yet**

4. **Fish Cable Using Ruler:**
   - Start at **light fixture box**
   - Remove light fixture to access ceiling box
   - From attic, push ruler/tape measure from light box toward fan box
   - **Tip:** Tie string to end of ruler so you can pull cable back
   - Once ruler reaches fan box, tape cable to ruler/string
   - Pull ruler back, bringing cable with it

5. **Alternative Method - Push from Fan Box:**
   - Start at fan box location
   - Push ruler toward light box
   - When ruler appears at light box, attach cable
   - Pull cable back to fan box

**Pro Tips for Short Runs:**
- For runs under 4 feet, stiff ruler works great
- For 4-8 feet, metal tape measure (locked extended) works well
- Tie string to ruler tip before fishing - makes pulling cable easier
- Work in good lighting - headlamp is essential
- Have helper shine flashlight from below if possible

#### Phase 3: Wiring Options Based on Light Configuration

**Option A: Tap Power at Light, Add Separate Fan Switch**

If existing light has constant power:

1. **At Light Box:**
   - Connect new cable to power source:
     - Black (new) to hot wire (constant power)
     - White (new) to neutral wire bundle
     - Ground to ground bundle
   - Run new cable to fan location
   - Run separate cable down wall to new fan switch location

2. **At Fan Box:**
   - Receive cable from light
   - Send cable to fan switch
   - Receive switched power back from fan switch
   - Connect to fan per manufacturer instructions

3. **New Fan Switch:**
   - Install old-work box in wall (not in door frame)
   - Wire switch to control fan

**Pros:**
- ✅ Separate fan control
- ✅ Professional installation
- ✅ Meets code

**Cons:**
- ❌ Requires fishing cable down wall for switch
- ❌ More complex wiring

2. **At Fan Box:**

## METHOD 1: Fishing Cable Through Ceiling (RECOMMENDED)

**Best For:** Clean, professional look with hidden wiring
**Difficulty:** Medium to Advanced
**Cost:** $150-300
**Time:** 4-8 hours per fan

### Why This Method?

This is the standard professional approach. While it requires more skill, it provides:
- ✅ Completely hidden wiring
- ✅ Code-compliant installation
- ✅ Adds home value
- ✅ Clean aesthetic
- ✅ No visible conduit or surface wiring

### Required Access Scenarios

#### Scenario A: Accessible Attic Above (EASIEST)

**If you have attic access above the bedrooms, this is your best option.**

**Step-by-Step Process:**

**Phase 1: Planning the Route**

1. **Locate the Fan Position:**
   - From below, measure from two walls to mark exact fan center location
   - Drive a small finish nail up through the ceiling at the center point
   - Go to attic and find the nail - this is your fan location

2. **Locate the Switch Position:**
   - Mark where you want the wall switch (typically 48" from floor, near door)
   - The switch should be on an interior wall if possible
   - Drive a long wire or coat hanger up through the wall at the switch box location

3. **Identify Power Source:**
   - **Option A:** Existing outlet in same room - tap into outlet box
   - **Option B:** Existing junction box in attic
   - **Option C:** Run cable from adjacent room outlet

**Phase 2: Installing the Ceiling Box**

1. **From Attic:**
   - Find your nail marking fan location
   - Position a ceiling fan-rated electrical box (must support 70+ lbs)
   - Install box **between joists** using:
     - Adjustable fan brace bar (easiest - about $15)
     - OR attach box directly to joist side with screws
   - **CRITICAL:** Never attach fan to drywall alone

2. **Cut Ceiling Opening:**
   - From below, use box as template to mark ceiling
   - Cut drywall carefully with drywall saw
   - Remove cutout and clean edges

**Phase 3: Running Cable**

1. **Choose Cable Type:**
   - **14/2 NM-B (Romex)** if tapping 15A circuit
   - **12/2 NM-B** if tapping 20A circuit
   - Must be rated for in-wall installation

2. **Route from Power Source to Ceiling Box:**
   - Lay cable along joists in attic
   - Staple cable within 8" of box and every 4.5 feet
   - Leave 8-10" of extra cable at ceiling box
   - Feed cable into ceiling box and secure with cable clamp

3. **Route from Ceiling Box to Switch:**
   - This is the tricky part - need to fish cable **down the wall**
   - From attic, drill hole through top plate above switch location
   - Feed cable down the wall cavity
   - Use fish tape or weighted string to guide cable to switch box location

**Phase 4: Installing the Switch Box**

1. **Cut Switch Opening:**
   - Use old-work (retrofit) electrical box
   - Trace box outline at switch location
   - Cut opening carefully with drywall saw

2. **Fish Cable to Switch:**
   - From attic, feed cable down through top plate hole
   - Use fish tape from switch opening to grab cable
   - Pull cable through switch opening
   - Secure cable in old-work box with built-in clamp
   - Install box in wall opening

**Phase 5: Making Connections**

1. **At Power Source (Existing Outlet/Junction):**
   - **⚠️ TURN OFF POWER AT BREAKER**
   - Verify power is off with voltage tester
   - Connect new cable:
     - Black (hot) to black wire
     - White (neutral) to white wire
     - Bare (ground) to ground wire
   - Use wire nuts rated for number of wires

2. **At Switch:**
   - Connect black wire from power source to one switch terminal (brass screw)
   - Connect black wire going to fan to other switch terminal
   - Connect white wires together with wire nut
   - Connect ground wires together and to switch green screw
   - **Alternative for fan with light:** Use 3-way switch or dual switch

3. **At Ceiling Fan Box:**
   - Leave wiring for fan installation
   - Cap wires with wire nuts until fan is installed
   - Follow fan manufacturer's wiring diagram

**Phase 6: Testing**

1. Restore power at breaker
2. Test switch operation with voltage tester at ceiling box
3. Install ceiling fan per manufacturer instructions
4. Final test of all functions

### Fishing Tips for Success

**Problem: Can't get cable down wall from attic**

**Solutions:**
- **Fish Tape Method:** Use two fish tapes - one from above, one from switch hole - hook them together
- **Weighted String:** Tie string to small bolt, drop from above, use magnet to guide to switch opening
- **Flex Bit:** Use extra-long flexible drill bit to drill from attic down to switch hole
- **Chain Drop:** Small chain falls straighter than string - easier to fish

**Problem: Fireblocking in wall cavity**

**Solutions:**
- Drill through fireblocking from attic using long drill bit
- Create small access holes in wall to drill through blocks, then patch
- Route around fireblocking through adjacent stud bay

**Problem: Insulation blocking view/access**

**Solutions:**
- Temporarily move insulation aside
- Use headlamp and camera phone to see in tight spaces
- Work in sections, moving insulation as you go

## METHOD 2: Surface-Mounted Raceway (BACKUP)

**Best For:** When fishing cable is impossible but you want permanent wiring
**Difficulty:** Easy to Medium
**Cost:** $80-150 per run
**Time:** 2-3 hours per fan

### Overview

Surface-mounted raceway (wire mold) is a code-compliant way to run wiring on wall/ceiling surfaces. Modern raceway can be painted to blend with walls.

### Advantages
- ✅ No fishing required
- ✅ Easy DIY installation
- ✅ Can be painted to match walls
- ✅ Code compliant
- ✅ Modifiable/removable later

### Disadvantages
- ❌ Visible on walls (even when painted)
- ❌ Not as clean as hidden wiring
- ❌ Can be damaged by furniture/impacts
- ❌ May look "added on" rather than original

### Installation Process

**Step 1: Plan Route**
1. Shortest path from power source (outlet) to ceiling fan location
2. Route along wall corners and trim where possible to minimize visibility
3. Cross ceiling along edge or in corner

**Step 2: Select Raceway System**
- **Legrand Wiremold** (most common)
- **Cordmate** (lower profile)
- Size: 3/4" or 1" channel for 14/2 cable

**Step 3: Install Base Track**
1. **⚠️ TURN OFF POWER** to source outlet
2. Snap off cover from raceway base
3. Measure and cut base pieces with hacksaw
4. Attach base to wall using:
   - Adhesive backing (if available)
   - Small screws into studs/anchors every 16-24"
5. Use corner fittings for turns

**Step 4: Run Wire**
1. Lay 14/2 NM cable in base channel
2. Wire should lie flat in channel
3. Leave extra wire at ends for connections

**Step 5: Make Connections**
1. **At source outlet:**
   - Remove outlet from box
   - Add new outlet or junction box extension
   - Connect raceway wire to outlet wires
   - Use box extender if needed to meet code
2. **At ceiling:**
   - Install surface-mount ceiling box at fan location
   - Run raceway to ceiling box
   - Connect wires inside box
3. **At switch:**
   - Install surface-mount switch box
   - Connect switch in line between power and fan

**Step 6: Install Covers**
1. Snap covers over base channel
2. Ensure covers are fully seated
3. Caulk gaps if desired
4. Prime and paint raceway to match wall

### Minimizing Visual Impact
- Run along baseboards, door trim, crown molding
- Paint to match wall color
- Use smaller profile raceway where possible
- Plan route through less visible areas (behind furniture, in corners)

## METHOD 3: Wireless Smart Switch System (BACKUP)

**Best For:** When you want wall switch control without running switch wiring
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Cost:** $100-200 per fan
**Time:** 1-2 hours per fan (after power is at ceiling)

### Overview

Run power directly to ceiling fan (easier - no switch leg required), then use wireless battery-powered wall switch.

### How It Works
1. Power runs from source directly to ceiling fan box (no switch in between)
2. Install smart ceiling fan (with WiFi/RF receiver)
3. Mount battery-powered wireless switch on wall (no wiring needed)
4. Wireless switch controls fan via radio signal

### Advantages
- ✅ Eliminates need to fish switch leg down wall
- ✅ "Switch" can be mounted anywhere (battery powered)
- ✅ No ugly doorframe switch issue
- ✅ Can add multiple switches in different locations
- ✅ Smart features (app control, scheduling, voice control)

### Disadvantages
- ❌ Requires compatible smart fan or add-on receiver
- ❌ Batteries need replacing (1-2 years typically)
- ❌ Depends on wireless technology (potential interference)
- ❌ Higher equipment cost

### Recommended Systems

**Option A: Hunter/Lutron Caseta**
- **Hunter SimpleConnect Ceiling Fan** (~$200-300)
- **Lutron Caseta Switch (battery)** (~$60)
- Works with Alexa, Google, HomeKit
- Very reliable RF system

**Option B: Bond Bridge System**
- **Bond Bridge** (~$100) - universal hub
- **Any RF ceiling fan** (your existing fans may work)
- **Battery-powered wireless switch** (~$25)
- Add smart control to non-smart fans

**Option C: Sonoff/Smart Life**
- **WiFi smart fan controller module** (~$20-30)
- Install module in fan canopy
- Use app or wireless button
- Budget-friendly option

### Installation Process

**Step 1: Run Power to Ceiling Box**
- Use Method 1 (fishing cable) but skip the switch leg
- OR use Method 2 (surface raceway) directly to ceiling
- Run hot, neutral, and ground only (no switch leg needed)

**Step 2: Install Smart Fan Module**
- If fan isn't smart: install receiver module in fan canopy
- Connect per manufacturer instructions
- Module intercepts power before fan motor/light

**Step 3: Mount Wireless Switch**
- Choose ideal wall location (standard switch height, 48" from floor)
- Mount switch base with screws or adhesive
- No wiring needed - battery powered
- Pair switch with receiver per instructions

**Step 4: Setup and Test**
- Test wireless switch operation
- Configure any smart features (app, voice control)
- Set up automation if desired

## METHOD 4: Remote-Only Control (SIMPLEST BACKUP)

**Best For:** Quick solution when all else fails
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Cost:** $0-50 (fans usually include remotes)
**Time:** 30 minutes per fan

### Overview

Your Mercury Row Trosclair fans include remote controls. Simply run power to ceiling (constant hot), control everything via remote.

### Advantages
- ✅ Simplest wiring (just hot + neutral + ground to ceiling)
- ✅ No wall switch needed at all
- ✅ Remote usually included with fan
- ✅ Can mount remote holder on wall like a switch

### Disadvantages
- ❌ Remote can be lost/misplaced
- ❌ Batteries need replacement
- ❌ No wall switch (non-traditional)
- ❌ May reduce home value/appeal

### Installation Process

**Step 1: Run Power to Ceiling Box**
- Use Method 1 or 2 to get constant power to ceiling
- No switch required - power always on
- Connect: Hot → Black, Neutral → White, Ground → Bare

**Step 2: Install Fan**
- Follow manufacturer instructions
- Fan's built-in receiver will control power

**Step 3: Mount Remote Holder**
- Install remote holder/cradle on wall at switch height
- Provides dedicated "home" for remote
- Looks more intentional than leaving remote on nightstand

## CODE COMPLIANCE & SAFETY

### National Electrical Code (NEC) Requirements

**Ceiling Fan Support:**
- Box MUST be rated for ceiling fan (70+ lbs minimum)
- NEVER hang fan from standard light box
- Must be secured to structural member (joist or fan-rated brace)

**Cable Installation:**
- NM cable must be stapled within 8" of box and every 4.5 feet
- Cannot run exposed where subject to physical damage
- Must maintain 1.25" depth from surface when in studs/joists
- Proper cable clamps required at all boxes

**Switch Requirements:**
- Single-pole switch for fan-only OR separate switches for fan + light
- Switch must be on hot (black) wire, never neutral
- Ground wire must be connected at all locations
- Switch must be accessible (not behind furniture)

**GFCI/AFCI:**
- Ceiling fans typically don't require GFCI
- May require AFCI depending on local code and circuit age
- Check with local building department

### Safety Warnings

**⚠️ ELECTRICAL HAZARDS:**
- **ALWAYS turn off power at breaker before working**
- **ALWAYS verify power is off with voltage tester**
- Never work on live circuits
- Never touch bare wire without verifying power is off

**⚠️ STRUCTURAL HAZARDS:**
- Work safely in attic - step only on joists, never insulation/ceiling
- Wear dust mask when working around insulation
- Watch for protruding nails and roof nails from above
- Use proper ladder/scaffolding when working overhead

**⚠️ DRILLING HAZARDS:**
- Always check for existing wiring before drilling
- Use stud finder with AC detection
- Drill slowly when penetrating unknown cavities
- Wear safety glasses when drilling overhead

### Permit Requirements

**When Permits Are Required:**
- Most jurisdictions require permit for new circuit wiring
- Permit usually NOT required for replacing existing fixtures
- Check with Oakland Building Department: (510) 238-3891

**Benefits of Pulling Permit:**
- Ensures code compliance
- Inspection verifies safe installation
- May be required for insurance claims
- Protects resale value

**Permit Process:**
1. Submit plan to building department
2. Pay permit fee (~$100-200)
3. Perform work
4. Call for inspection
5. Receive approval and close permit

## WHEN TO CALL A PROFESSIONAL

**Call a licensed electrician if:**

- ❌ You're not comfortable working with electricity
- ❌ Your electrical panel is full (no available breakers)
- ❌ You encounter aluminum wiring
- ❌ Existing wiring appears damaged or non-standard
- ❌ You can't locate a safe power source to tap
- ❌ Fishing cable proves impossible after multiple attempts
- ❌ You want permitted work with guaranteed code compliance
- ❌ Your insurance requires licensed electrical work

**Cost to Hire Electrician:**
- Ceiling fan wiring installation: $300-600 per location
- Includes: running cable, installing boxes, switch, testing
- Permit typically included in electrician's quote
- Usually completed in 2-4 hours per fan

**Finding a Good Electrician:**
- Get 3 quotes minimum
- Verify California C-10 license (Electrical Contractor)
- Check CSLB.ca.gov for complaints
- Ask if price includes permit
- Request references from recent jobs
